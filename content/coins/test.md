### Historical & Numismatic Context

Struck by a military mint traveling with Julius Caesar at the outbreak of the Great Roman Civil War in 49 BC. This is arguably the most famous and widely recognized of all Roman coins.

**Obverse Iconography:**  
Features an elephant advancing right, trampling a horned serpent (often interpreted as a dragon or carnyx, representing Caesar's victory over the Gauls) with the name "CAESAR" boldly in the exergue. This was the first coin issued in open defiance of the Senate, bearing the name of a private individual. 

**Reverse Iconography:**  
Displays the emblems of the pontificate (the high priesthood held by Caesar): a *simpulum* (ladle), *aspergillum* (sprinkler), *securis* (sacrificial axe surmounted by a wolf's head), and an *apex* (priest's hat). These implements highlight Caesar's role as *Pontifex Maximus*, legitimizing his authority through religious office.
